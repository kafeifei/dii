# English naming rules

The game "烬渊" (English title: Ember Abyss) replaces every Diablo II proper noun with its own name, in Chinese (`style.md`) and in English. Each row of a names file (`<category>.json`) has `name`, our Chinese name, and `nameEn`, our English name; `en` is Blizzard's English and `official` the official Chinese it was written against. `node tools/d2-import.mjs` only takes a row that has both names, lists the rest in `report.md`, and stops on a `nameEn` that reads as Blizzard's English (hard rule 1) or is not plain ASCII; `node tools/d2-validate.mjs` checks the rules below on the generated data.

## Goal
- **Known at a glance.** A player who knows Diablo II reads the name and says which item, monster or place it stands for, without the base, the stats or the tooltip. They know it by the most telling part of Blizzard's name: its key word (the Wind of Windforce), its image (the oak of Heart of the Oak), the root of a monster family's name (Fallen), the sound of a person's or place's name (Tal Rasha). That part stays in ours.
- **But not Blizzard's.** Change only what does not stand in the way: a synonym or near-synonym for one word, another modifier, phrasing or word order, a word added, another word for the thing. One word changed is enough, as long as the change is none of the near copies of hard rule 1. Stop there: a name a veteran cannot place fails as surely as a copy.
- **Blizzard's name is the anchor.** Every row keeps the English (`en`) and the Chinese (`official`) it was written against; keep both. Write and review against them: find the word a veteran knows the row by and keep it, then check that the change is enough (rule 1) and the name still known (rule 2). When changing a name of ours, hold it against Blizzard's again, not against our last version, or each round takes it further away.
- **One name in two languages.** Our English and our Chinese name the same thing with the same image where they can, and each is held to its own official name: our Chinese against the official Chinese (`style.md`), our English against Blizzard's English. Mostly one choice serves both, the official Chinese being a translation of the English: 不朽之君 keeps 不朽, and "Immortal Sovereign" keeps Immortal. Where the official Chinese went its own way, each keeps what its own players know: Buriza-Do Kyanon is 暴雪重炮 in Chinese, so our Chinese keeps 雪 and 炮 (雪崩巨炮) and our English the sound of Buriza and Kyanon; "Avalanche Bombard" follows our Chinese, and no English player would place it.
- **One word for one word.** Where the same Chinese word recurs (钩爪 in a rare name and in a monster name), use the same English word, in every names file: 坚盾 is "Escutcheon" as a rare word and in a monster name, 獠牙 "Tusk" there and in the uniques; and one English word stands for one Chinese word: "Rampart" is 垣, so 坚盾 is not "Rampart", "Iron" is 铁, so 钢 is never "Iron", and "haunt" is 祟 ("Fox-Haunted"). The word must also fit every row it is in: 烈日 is "Dayblaze", since the 烈日 of 东升烈日 is a rising sun and no "Noonblaze". A form of the word counts ("Sear" and "Searing" for 焚灼). A word of Blizzard's name for the row is the one to keep, not to avoid: 钢 is "Steel" and 鲸 "Whale" in the rows Blizzard named with them as everywhere else, so 沧鲸之, of the Whale, keeps Whale. Where Blizzard's word and these rules pull apart, the Chinese word changes so that both hold, not the English away from Blizzard's.
- **Tone.** Dark, gothic, old-world, weighty. No modern words, no jokes, no puns on Blizzard names. An item names a thing, not a process ("Thousandfold Forging" for Steel).

## Hard rules
1. **Not Blizzard's, nor a near copy of it.** Each of these counts as unchanged, and so do any of them together:
   - Blizzard's English itself, compared case-insensitively, "The", apostrophes, spaces and hyphens aside ("Wind Force" is Windforce);
   - it with only "of", "the", "a", "an" or a possessive "'s" added, dropped or moved, or with its words in another order: "Force of the Wind" for Windforce, "Oak Heart" for Heart of the Oak;
   - one word respelled so that it reads the same or nearly, a letter or two changed, added or dropped ("Haenna" for Hannah, "Pock" for Pox), or put in another form ("of Freezing Arrow" for of Freezing Arrows): the English for a homophone. A transliteration that respells Blizzard's name so is one too ("Kashia" for Kashya).

   Nor may a whole name (an item, set, runeword, monster, area, NPC or hireling) be the English of any other row Blizzard named ("Exile" is a runeword). `tools/d2-import.mjs` stops on the first two against the row's own English. `tools/d2-validate.mjs` fails them too, and a whole name that is another row's English (case, "The", apostrophes, spaces and hyphens aside); it prints a warning for every name within edit distance 2 of its row's English after that comparison, the glossary's transliterations taken out of both first (a name that only swaps the person, "Weirte's Leg" for Wirt's Leg, is listed unless our Chinese did the same), and for every English form of the glossary within edit distance 2 of Blizzard's name. Change each one unless it is plainly another word ("Rime" for Ice).
2. **Known at a glance.** The word a veteran knows the row by (its key word, image or root, the sound of a name) stays, or a near-synonym nobody can miss takes its place. Not known: a new image ("Soul of the Warboar" for Heart of Wolverine); every word swapped, only the structure left ("Graven Sovereign" for Immortal King, "Galechaser's Wrath" for Windforce); a word only a dictionary knows ("Soul of the Glutton", the glutton being an old name for the wolverine); a riddle ("Willow-Piercer's" for Archer's, after a Chinese saying); our Chinese followed where it left Blizzard's English ("Avalanche Bombard" for Buriza-Do Kyanon). Known: "Windmight" for Windforce, "Immortal Sovereign" for Immortal King, "Quill Vermin" for Quill Rat, "Bitter Rain" for Rain, "Bloodmire Heath" for Blood Moor. Review with `en` and `official` covered: a name counts when Blizzard's comes to mind at once.
3. **No names of other famous works, nor brands.** Avoid names from other famous games, books or films, and real brands (Hibiki is a whisky, and "Hibiko" a letter from it; Shanling a hi-fi maker, Otone a maker of speakers). Look hardest where a name keeps Blizzard's word and changes the other: "Windfury" (World of Warcraft) and "Galeforce" (League of Legends) each keep half of Windforce.
4. **No duplicates**, names that differ only in spaces and hyphens counting as one ("Twinshade", "Twin-Shade"). None across uniques, set items, sets and runewords, and none inside any other list (affixes, rare words, monsters, areas, hirelings, the monster name words), except for rows of one D2 string, which share theirs. Quest bases carry their unique's name on purpose (`bases.json` hfh, hst, qf1, qf2). Prefer a word that is not Blizzard's name for another row, a runeword ("Bulwark"), a unique, a monster or an affix ("Slaughter"), in a name word too, or a veteran thinks of that row: "the Lacerator" would carry the unique axe Lacerator into a monster title. The validator lists every name word (an affix, a rare word, a monster name word) that is the whole English of a unique, set, runeword, monster, area or NPC.
5. **Use the glossary for transliterated names.** Every person, place, demon or faction name uses the English form in `glossary-en.json` (`newEn`, next to the Chinese `new` of `glossary.json`): where our Chinese name has 何罗德门, the English has "Heroldmen". No English name keeps Blizzard's (Horadric, Akara, Zakarum, …). The English form is heard against Blizzard's name, not against our Chinese: it keeps the sound ("Thol Lashar" for Tal Rasha, "Heroldmen" for Horadrim) and is no respelling of it (rule 1); a romanised Chinese that has lost it ("Yajalo", from 亚珈萝, for Akara) fails rule 2. A new transliteration goes into both glossaries.
6. **Title Case**, plain ASCII, every part of a hyphenated word too ("Death's-Head"; a small word inside stays lowercase, as in "Man-at-Arms"). Only a magic suffix starts with a lowercase "of" and only a monster title with "the". Apostrophes and hyphens are fine. About 1–4 words, five at most where an "of the" takes two ("Mantle of the Stone Ram"); the validator fails a sixth.

## Per category
- **Magic prefixes**: an adjective or adjectival noun before the base name, as in "Jagged Long Sword" → our "Serrated …". One word or a short phrase, never "of".
- **Magic suffixes**: start with "of", as in "of Warding". Automagic rows follow the table they take their name from.
- **Rare name words**: the first list is nouns or images ("Beast", "Raven"), the second item-type or action nouns ("Bite", "Song", "Hold"); a rare item is "Raven Bite". One word each.
- **Sets**: the person part comes from the glossary and is the same in the set name and all its pieces. Pieces follow "<Name>'s <Thing>".
- **Monsters**: generic nouns such as "Zombie" and "Skeleton" may not stay as they are either (they are Blizzard's English); D2 coinages such as "Fallen", "Quill Rat" and "Moon Clan" change too, keeping the word they are known by ("Quill Vermin"). Superuniques are people or titles: person-name rules.
- **Random unique monster names**: prefix and suffix one word each, the title "the …" goes last: "Murk Canker the Sledge". The suffix keeps its slot's grammar: a noun ("Canker"), not a verb ("Loll" for 长舌 became "Slander"), or an agent noun where the Chinese is 「X者」 ("Eviscerator").
- **Areas, NPCs, quests**: change in the same spirit as the Chinese name ("Blood Moor" → "Bloodmire Heath"). People use the glossary.
- **Hirelings** (`mercenaries.json`): personal names, short, gothic, each its own, heard against Blizzard's hireling name like any transliteration (hard rule 5), spelled the Western way rather than as Mandarin pinyin syllable by syllable ("Peiji", "Kaier"): 艾黎札 → "Aelizeth", 嫚赫拉 → "Manhilde". Keep them at least three letters (edit distance 3) from Blizzard's name for any hireling and from our other hirelings' (the validator checks it: "Hrimna" was two from Oriana). The two Japanese ones keep a Japanese name, read from our kanji (真绘子 → "Makie", for Maeko), as far from a brand as from a hireling ("Hibiko" was a letter from the whisky Hibiki, and "Otone", 響 read oto and 子 ne, a speaker maker's name); where no reading of the kanji makes a good name, the kanji change (响子 became 杏子). A name has the syllables of our Chinese and no more (寒娜 is "Hwenna", not "Haelwenne"), and no more than two of them share an ending, their last three letters (seven once ended in "-nne"; the validator checks it).
- **Skills** (`skills.json`): only the proper-noun-ish ones, as in Chinese; a skill named by a runeword's string takes the runeword's names.
- **Runes** are numbered: "Rune N", made by the importer.
- **Generic rows** (`bases.json` with `"generic": true`: generic words D2CE has no Chinese string for, like Scroll of Resistance) have only the Chinese and keep Blizzard's English.
