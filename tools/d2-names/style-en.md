# English naming rules

The game "烬渊" (English title: Ember Abyss) replaces every Diablo II proper noun with its own name, in Chinese (`style.md`) and in English. Each row of a names file (`<category>.json`) has `name`, our Chinese name, and `nameEn`, our English name; `en` is Blizzard's English and `official` the official Chinese it was written against. `node tools/d2-import.mjs` only takes a row that has both names, lists the rest in `report.md`, and stops on a `nameEn` that is Blizzard's English or not plain ASCII; `node tools/d2-validate.mjs` checks the rules below on the generated data.

## Goal
- **Similar but different.** A veteran player should be able to tell which original item, monster or place it stands for, but it must not be Blizzard's English name. It must also not be that name with one letter or one word swapped for a near-synonym that reads the same, for example "Windforce" → "Wind Force"; nor a respelling a letter or two away ("Haenna" for Hannah, "Pock" for Pox); nor the plain English for the same thing ("Porcupine" for Quill Rat) or for Blizzard's word ("Mettle", first a spelling of metal, for Steel); nor Blizzard's whole name with a word added ("Bitter Rain" for Rain); nor its structure with each word swapped for a synonym ("Soul of the Glutton" for Heart of Wolverine, "Aeon King" for Immortal King, "of Hoarfrost Shafts" for of Freezing Arrow), however the words are ordered ("Spectre's Lurking Tread" for Wraithstep, "Verdict of Anguish" for The Gavel of Pain): at least one image must be our own. Where our Chinese is itself such a swap (貂熊之魄 for 狼獾之心, 凝霜矢 for 冻结箭), the Chinese changes too, and its note says so.
- **Follow our Chinese name.** The English name carries the same image as our Chinese `name`, not the old official one. Example: our Chinese 逐风之怒 becomes "Galechaser's Wrath". It need not be a literal translation; it should read like a natural fantasy name. Where the same Chinese word recurs (钩爪 in a rare name and in a monster name), use the same English word, in every names file: 坚盾 is "Escutcheon" as a rare word and in a monster name, 獠牙 "Tusk" there and in the uniques; and one English word stands for one Chinese word: "Rampart" is 垣, so 坚盾 is not "Rampart", and "Iron" is 铁, so 钢 is never "Iron" (Steelclash's 钢铁 became 金铁 for "Chorus of Iron"), and "haunt" is 祟 ("Fox-Haunted"; 幽尸 became 祟尸 for "Corpsehaunt"). The word must also fit every row it is in: 烈日 is "Dayblaze", since the 烈日 of 东升烈日 is a rising sun and no "Noonblaze". A form of the word counts ("Sear" and "Searing" for 焚灼). Where that word is in Blizzard's name for the row, the row takes another, and a form counts here too: 水魅罗网 is "Drownwraith Net", because "Snare" is in "Kelpie Snare"; 震怒 is "Fury", but the prefix Furious takes "Seething". The other word must not hold Blizzard's either ("Frostiron" for Iron), and the word the Chinese has elsewhere is the one to take: 枯骨 is "Marrowless" in the prefixes, so Boneshade's 枯骨幽影 is "Marrowless Umbra", not a "Witherbone" that keeps Bone.
- **Tone.** Dark, gothic, old-world, weighty. No modern words, no jokes, no puns on Blizzard names. Avoid names from other famous games, books or films, and real brands (Hibiki is a whisky, and "Hibiko" a letter from it; Shanling a hi-fi maker, Otone a maker of speakers). Prefer a word that is not Blizzard's name for something else, a runeword ("Bulwark"), a unique, a monster or an affix ("Slaughter"), in a name word too: "the Lacerator" would carry the unique axe Lacerator into a monster title. An item names a thing, not a process ("Thousandfold Forging" for Steel).

## Hard rules
1. **Must differ from `en`.** Compared case-insensitively, ignoring "The", apostrophes, spaces and hyphens. A one-word synonym swap that keeps the same structure and meaning does not count as different. A whole name (an item, set, runeword, monster, area, NPC or hireling) must not be the English of any other row Blizzard named either ("Exile" is a runeword). `tools/d2-validate.mjs` prints a warning for every name within edit distance 2 of its row's English after that comparison, the glossary's transliterations taken out of both first (a name that only swaps the person, "Weirte's Leg" for Wirt's Leg, is listed unless our Chinese did the same), and for every name holding its row's whole English with a word added, the transliterations out of both again ("Batwing" for Wing, "of Sinfire" for of Fire, "Ishenhed's Twin Horns" for Isenhart's Horns); change each one unless it is plainly another word ("Rime" for Ice). It also lists every name word (an affix, a rare word, a monster name word) that is the whole English of a unique, set, runeword, monster, area or NPC.
2. **Use the glossary for transliterated names.** Every person, place, demon or faction name uses the English form in `glossary-en.json` (`newEn`, next to the Chinese `new` of `glossary.json`): where our Chinese name has 何罗德门, the English has "Heroldmen". No English name keeps Blizzard's (Horadric, Akara, Zakarum, …). A new transliteration goes into both glossaries.
3. **No duplicates**, names that differ only in spaces and hyphens counting as one ("Twinshade", "Twin-Shade"). None across uniques, set items, sets and runewords, and none inside any other list (affixes, rare words, monsters, areas, hirelings, the monster name words), except for rows of one D2 string, which share theirs. Quest bases carry their unique's name on purpose (`bases.json` hfh, hst, qf1, qf2).
4. **Title Case**, plain ASCII, every part of a hyphenated word too ("Death's-Head"; a small word inside stays lowercase, as in "Man-at-Arms"). Only a magic suffix starts with a lowercase "of" and only a monster title with "the". Apostrophes and hyphens are fine. About 1–4 words, five at most where an "of the" takes two ("Mantle of the Stone Ram"); the validator fails a sixth.

## Per category
- **Magic prefixes**: an adjective or adjectival noun before the base name, as in "Jagged Long Sword" → our "Serrated …". One word or a short phrase, never "of".
- **Magic suffixes**: start with "of", as in "of Warding". Automagic rows follow the table they take their name from.
- **Rare name words**: the first list is nouns or images ("Beast", "Raven"), the second item-type or action nouns ("Bite", "Song", "Hold"); a rare item is "Raven Bite". One word each.
- **Sets**: the person part comes from the glossary and is the same in the set name and all its pieces. Pieces follow "<Name>'s <Thing>".
- **Monsters**: generic nouns such as "Zombie" and "Skeleton" may not stay as they are either (they are Blizzard's English); D2 coinages such as "Fallen", "Quill Rat" and "Moon Clan" change too. Superuniques are people or titles: person-name rules.
- **Random unique monster names**: prefix and suffix one word each, the title "the …" goes last: "Murk Canker the Sledge". The suffix keeps its slot's grammar: a noun ("Canker"), not a verb ("Loll" for 长舌 became "Slander"), or an agent noun where the Chinese is 「X者」 ("Eviscerator").
- **Areas, NPCs, quests**: change in the same spirit as the Chinese name ("Blood Moor" → "Bloodmire Heath"). People use the glossary.
- **Hirelings** (`mercenaries.json`): personal names, short, gothic, each its own, after our Chinese transliteration in the glossary's manner (Akara 亚珈萝 → "Yajalo"), spelled the Western way rather than as Mandarin pinyin syllable by syllable ("Peiji", "Kaier"): 艾黎札 → "Aelizeth", 嫚赫拉 → "Manhilde". Keep them at least three letters (edit distance 3) from Blizzard's name for any hireling and from our other hirelings' (the validator checks it: "Hrimna" was two from Oriana). The two Japanese ones keep a Japanese name, read from our kanji (杏子, one way to write Kyoko, → "Anzu", its other reading; 真绘子 → "Makie"), as far from a brand as from a hireling ("Hibiko" was a letter from the whisky Hibiki, and "Otone", 響 read oto and 子 ne, a speaker maker's name); where no reading of the kanji makes a good name, the kanji change (响子 became 杏子). A name has the syllables of our Chinese and no more (寒娜 is "Hwenna", not "Haelwenne"), and no more than two of them share an ending, their last three letters (seven once ended in "-nne"; the validator checks it).
- **Skills** (`skills.json`): only the proper-noun-ish ones, as in Chinese; a skill named by a runeword's string takes the runeword's names.
- **Runes** are numbered: "Rune N", made by the importer.
- **Generic rows** (`bases.json` with `"generic": true`: generic words D2CE has no Chinese string for, like Scroll of Resistance) have only the Chinese and keep Blizzard's English.
